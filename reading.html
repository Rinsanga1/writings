Reading List

Based on the 1952 Great Books of the Western World (54-volume set), compiled by Robert Maynard Hutchins and Mortimer Adler.

This set presents foundational Western texts in roughly chronological order. The first three volumes are introductory/reference:

  • Vol. 1: The Great Conversation (Hutchins essay on liberal education)
  • Vols. 2–3: Syntopicon (topical index of 102 Great Ideas — use as a reference tool, not primary reading)

Suggested Approaches to Reading

  • Chronological / Volume-by-Volume: Follow the set's order for historical flow.
  • Thematic: Use the Syntopicon to explore ideas like Justice, Liberty, or Nature across authors.
  • 10-Year Plan: Adler and the editors created structured yearly reading guides (often 15–20 selections per year). These are selective excerpts rather than full volumes. Many online resources adapt this.

Core Reading List by Major Works / Volumes (1952 Edition)

Focus on these highlights. Many are complete or near-complete in the volumes:

Ancient Foundations

  • Homer: Iliad, Odyssey (Vol. 4)
  • Greek Drama: Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides, Aristophanes (selected plays, Vol. 5)
  • Herodotus: Histories; Thucydides: Peloponnesian War (Vol. 6)
  • Plato: Major dialogues (incl. Republic, Apology, Symposium, etc., Vol. 7)
  • Aristotle: Logic, Physics, Metaphysics, Ethics, Politics, Poetics, etc. (Vols. 8–9)
  • Hippocrates & Galen (medical classics, Vol. 10)
  • Euclid, Archimedes, Apollonius, Nicomachus (mathematics, Vol. 11)

Classical & Roman

  • Lucretius, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius (Vol. 12)
  • Virgil (Vol. 13)
  • Plutarch: Lives (Vol. 14)
  • Tacitus (Vol. 15)

Science, Philosophy & Early Christianity

  • Ptolemy, Copernicus, Kepler (astronomy, Vol. 16)
  • Plotinus (Vol. 17)
  • Augustine: Confessions, City of God (Vol. 18)
  • Aquinas: Summa Theologica (selections, Vols. 19–20)

Medieval to Renaissance

  • Dante: Divine Comedy (Vol. 21)
  • Chaucer (Vol. 22)
  • Machiavelli: The Prince; Hobbes: Leviathan (Vol. 23)
  • Rabelais: Gargantua and Pantagruel (Vol. 24)
  • Montaigne: Essays (Vol. 25)
  • Shakespeare (plays & sonnets, Vols. 26–27)

Early Modern Science & Literature

  • Gilbert, Galileo, Harvey (scientific method & biology, Vol. 28)
  • Cervantes: Don Quixote (Vol. 29)
  • Bacon (Vol. 30)
  • Descartes & Spinoza (Vol. 31)
  • Milton (Vol. 32)
  • Pascal (Vol. 33)
  • Newton & Huygens (Vol. 34)
  • Locke, Berkeley, Hume (Vol. 35)
  • Swift & Sterne (Vol. 36)
  • Fielding: Tom Jones (Vol. 37)

Enlightenment & Modern

  • Montesquieu & Rousseau (Vol. 38)
  • Adam Smith: Wealth of Nations (Vol. 39)
  • Gibbon: Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire (Vols. 40–41)
  • Kant (major critiques, Vol. 42)
  • American State Papers, Federalist, J.S. Mill (Vol. 43)
  • Boswell: Life of Johnson (Vol. 44)
  • Lavoisier, Fourier, Faraday (chemistry & physics, Vol. 45)
  • Hegel (Vol. 46)
  • Goethe: Faust (Vol. 47)
  • Melville: Moby-Dick (Vol. 48)
  • Darwin (Vol. 49)
  • Marx & Engels (Vol. 50)
  • Tolstoy: War and Peace (Vol. 51)
  • Dostoevsky: The Brothers Karamazov (Vol. 52)
  • William James: Principles of Psychology (Vol. 53)
  • Freud (selected psychoanalytic works, Vol. 54)

Tips for Getting Started

  1. Begin with Homer, Plato, and Shakespeare — they're foundational and rewarding.
  2. Alternate between literature, philosophy, history, and science to avoid burnout.
  3. Many works are free online (Project Gutenberg, Internet Archive) or in modern translations.
  4. For guided pacing, search for "Great Books 10-Year Reading Plan" or Adler's Great Ideas Program.
